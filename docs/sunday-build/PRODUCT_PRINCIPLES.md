# Sunday Product Principles

## Brand promise

**Progress feels better together.**

Sunday is an emotionally warm progress and support app. It helps people move toward things that matter while having someone they care about in their corner.

The primary relationship use case is couples. Sunday also supports close friends, family, and personally directed goals.

Sunday should feel warm, calm, focused, human, supportive, emotionally intelligent, tactile, visually satisfying, simple, and premium without feeling clinical.

Sunday should not feel like a traditional productivity dashboard, corporate task manager, Jira, Notion, a habit-streak machine, a gamified pressure system, or a social-network feed.

## Emotional loop

Progress → noticed → supported → motivation → more progress.

Accountability should happen without pressure, nagging, or shame.

## Home

Home answers: **“What matters right now?”**

Home is not where the user manages every goal. Current Focus dominates so the user can quickly understand what they are moving toward and the next useful step. Other goals remain accessible but visually quiet. Sunday reduces overwhelm rather than displaying everything simply because the data exists. Intentional whitespace is encouraged.

## Goals

Sunday does not merely store goals; it helps users make progress:

Goal → Sunday understands it → Sunday proposes useful small steps → one Next Step is surfaced → progress advances naturally.

Generated steps should be specific, actionable, contextual, human, small enough to start, and meaningful enough to matter. Avoid generic productivity wording.

## Relationship modes

Keep these human models distinct:

- **Just Me:** The user is moving toward something personally.
- **Together:** Two people are genuinely working toward the goal together.
- **Support Me / Supported:** The goal belongs to one person. Another person is in their corner and can notice or support progress without owning the work.

Do not collapse these modes into generic sharing permissions.

## Motion

Motion communicates continuity, state, progress, physicality, and acknowledgement. It should feel buttery, direct, tactile, restrained, and intentional. Avoid animation used purely as decoration.

Feedback should scale with the importance of the event:

- Small step completed → subtle satisfaction
- Multiple progress moments → richer acknowledgement
- Goal completed → stronger celebration
- Day or flow cleared → calm emotional reward

## UI rules

1. One screen, one primary purpose.
2. One obvious primary action.
3. Importance determines visual scale.
4. Show the next useful thing, not every available piece of information.
5. Use direct manipulation when an interaction has a physical metaphor.
6. Motion communicates state, not decoration.
7. Empty states help the user move forward.
8. Progress should feel good.

## Design language

Use a warm vanilla or cream foundation, Sunday coral for primary brand and action color, muted category colors, warm yellow accents, and soft lavender where useful. Favor rounded geometry, restrained borders, minimal shadows, confident typography, deliberate spacing, generous breathing room, and very little unnecessary chrome.

Avoid generic SaaS cards everywhere, heavy shadows, dashboard density, excessive pills or borders, unnecessary progress bars, and arbitrary decoration. Typography and spacing should carry much of the hierarchy.
